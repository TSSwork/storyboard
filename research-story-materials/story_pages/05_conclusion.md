# 05 - What did we learn?

**Act:** The bigger picture · **Time:** about 1 minute, then questions

## What to do on this page
1. Pause on the question card. Ask the audience: "Which vegetable do you think rain helps most?" Click the pill for their guess (or ask a volunteer to click it).
2. Click **Wanna see the conclusion?** A strip above the cards tells them whether their guess was right (cabbage gained most). Soft confetti plays and three cards come in one after another.
3. Read the large number on each card, then the heading. Leave the page up during questions.
4. **Hide again** puts the button back if you want to repeat the reveal. The confetti plays only on a click, never when you come back to the page.

## What to say
"Let's see if you were right. … Three things.

One: rain helps some vegetables. For cabbage, brinjal and carrot, knowing last month's rain made next month's forecast 8 to 20 percent better, and that survives strict checks.

Two: not all, and not precisely. The other four gained nothing we can trust, and even the best forecasts still miss by about a quarter of the price.

Three: this is a research finding about *where* rain carries information. It is not a price app. The next step is to test it on months that have not been published yet, and to use rain measured where the vegetables actually grow."

## Key points
- The answer is "for some vegetables", and that variation is the finding.
- The honest limits are stated on the slide itself, not hidden.

## If an evaluator asks
- **Where the numbers come from:** computed live from the stored results (Dambulla, rain across the whole island). "8–20%" is the range of RMSE gains for the three vegetables labelled *supported (corrected)*. "~25%" is the median, across the seven vegetables, of the rain model's mean absolute percentage error.
- **Limitations:** the test window was seen in the pilot, so this is exploratory; the rain series are national or box averages, not growing-area rain; the 62 forecast errors are serially dependent; prices are nominal.
- **Next steps:** a genuine out-of-sample test on newly published months; growing-area rainfall; a CPI/NCPI deflator. Horizons beyond one month would need rainfall *forecasts*, which is a separate study.
- **Why the guess and the confetti?** Committing to a guess makes people pay attention to the answer, and the confetti gives the talk a clear ending. The text stays as cautious as the evidence, and a wrong guess is answered honestly (for example, "no gain we can trust").

## What not to claim
- "Rain predicts vegetable prices." Say instead: "for three of seven vegetables, past rain improves one-month-ahead forecasts."
- Any causal effect of rain on prices.
