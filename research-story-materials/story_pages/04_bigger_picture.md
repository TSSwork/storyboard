# 04 - Rain is only one piece

**Act:** The bigger picture · **Time:** about 1½ minutes

## What to do on this page
1. Read the three KPI cards across the top, one sentence each.
2. Hover a dot in the left chart, the coral bar in the middle chart and the 2022 jump in the right chart.
3. Say clearly that these three are shown side by side on purpose and not added together.

## What to say
"Vegetable prices are only part of the food story. Three other forces matter.

First, weather and rice. In growing seasons that were wetter than usual, a larger share of the sown paddy land was harvested. Over 16 seasons the correlation is about plus 0.68. That is a link, not proof.

Second, imports. In bad years Sri Lanka buys a lot of rice from abroad, up to about 30 percent of supply in 2017. In normal years it is only a few percent.

Third, the rupee. A US dollar cost about 190 rupees at the start of 2021 and about 333 now, after the 2022 crisis. That makes everything imported more expensive.

These are three separate pressures. I am not combining them into one score."

## Key points
- Weather, import exposure and the exchange rate are separate dimensions of food-system vulnerability.
- None of them feeds the vegetable forecasting test.

## If an evaluator asks
- **Weather and paddy:** Yala season rain (March–August, ERA5-Land national) against the harvested/sown area ratio (DCS), both detrended with a linear trend, 2010–2025. Short series with policy shocks, so association only.
- **Import share:** FAO rice imports ÷ (domestic rice equivalent + imports). Rice equivalent = paddy × 0.67. The 0.67 conversion factor is a project assumption, not data. This is an *apparent* share: stocks, waste, seed and feed are not modelled, so it is not a self-sufficiency rate.
- **Exchange rate:** monthly mean of the CBSL indicative USD/LKR spot rate. Descriptive only; no pass-through to prices is estimated, because that needs a CPI/NCPI deflator.

## What not to claim
- That rain explains rice imports, or that the exchange rate explains vegetable prices.
- Any combined "vulnerability index".
